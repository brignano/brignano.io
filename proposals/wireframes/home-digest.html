Make the home page one short story

You're right that it feels like a lot. The page isn't too long because you have too much to say. It's long because it says the same few things five times, and every section gets the same giant heading, so nothing tells a reader what matters.

My recommendation: cut the home page from nine sections to four. Lead with who you are, show three pieces of work you're proud of, then your side projects, then a warm sign-off with one good photo. Everything that's cut already has a better home on /resume, /work or /coding, so nothing is lost. On a phone the page drops from about 12 screens to about 3½.

What I found

Measured on the current main branch at 390px and 1440px wide.

12 screens

That's how far you scroll on a phone (10,410px, about 1,160 words). Desktop is about 8.5 screens. Most personal sites people actually finish are 3 to 5.

“4,000+” × 6

The same claim appears six times. "40+ days" appears four times, "one command builds any project" twice, the status page twice. Highlights and Current Role say nearly the same thing back to back, so the second one reads as a re-run.

9 sections

Each has the same 36 to 48px heading and the same tall padding, so the page has no rhythm. Hero, About, Life, Highlights, Role, Work, Projects, Open source and Contact all look equally important, so a reader can't skim.

Tap to decode

Highlights needs a "tap any tag to see what it means" note. If a section needs a glossary, it's written for the wrong reader to be on the home page. Your case studies say the same things in plain words.

6 photo tiles

Why they don't land: they take a full phone screen (more than the hero on desktop), but the one-word captions say less than the sentence above them. Hiking, Traveling and Exploring look alike. It reads like a gallery, not like you.

3 audiences, mixed

Family content sits between About and Highlights, recruiter content is split across three sections, and engineer-only content (commit feed, contribution graph, stack list) is spread through the bottom half.

Keep the person-first hero from October. Fold the four work sections into one, and give the personal side a better spot than a grid.
