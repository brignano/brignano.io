Make the coding page easy to digest

One big number with one confident line under it, your top five languages, and a GitHub section that is one commit row plus the six-month graph from PR #350. The cards that only describe your setup go, and so does the paragraph that apologises for the numbers.

What makes it hard to read today

7 cards

stacked one after another: the hero, languages, editors, operating systems, activity types, last commit and the contribution graph. All of them have the same weight, so nothing says what matters.

2 apologies

open the page. The hero says GitHub "shows only a few hundred commits", then a second paragraph explains why the hours have slowed. That's the first thing a visitor reads.

3 setup cards

Editors, Operating Systems and Activity Types describe your tools, not your work. A recruiter or a friend learns nothing from them.

1 big commit card

Last commit takes a whole card: a button, the full commit body, the SHA, the file count, an avatar and your username. It's one fact.

Proposed page

Numbers and the commit are made-up samples: the live page fills them from WakaTime and GitHub. Tap Show all languages to see what sits behind the link. On desktop the hero sits on one row with "Now building" beside it, and Languages and GitHub sit side by side.

Choices behind it

  • One number, said plainly. The WakaTime total stays the headline. Under it is one line: tracked since Dec 2020 across every machine, and most of it is private work that never reaches GitHub. That's the same fact as the old "tip of the iceberg" copy, said without defending it.
  • Three facts in a row. Daily average, best day and tracking-since become one line of text instead of a labelled stat strip.
  • "Now building" moves up into the hero, so the page says what you're working on before it shows any charts.
  • Top five languages. Bars with hours, and "Show all languages" opens the rest. No pie chart.
  • Editors, Operating Systems and Activity Types are removed. They're still on your WakaTime profile, which the credits line links to.
  • Last commit is one row: the subject, the repo, how long ago and the +/− count, linking to the commit. The commit body, SHA chip, avatar and "Browse files" button go.
  • The graph is PR #350's six months, with no card around it, under the commit.
  • Cards become plain sections with 22–24px headings, matching home, projects and resume.
