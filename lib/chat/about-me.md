# About Anthony

## In a sentence

Anthony is a platform engineer who treats developer experience as a product:
he builds the paved roads, tooling and CI/CD systems that let thousands of
engineers ship without thinking about the plumbing.

## What he's known for at work

- Running The Hartford's internal developer platform end to end — the CLI,
  desktop app and IDE extensions engineers use every day, and the CI/CD and
  DevSecOps toolchain underneath them.
- Turning platform data into decisions: the DevOps intelligence layer streams
  pipeline and tooling telemetry so the team can see where developers are
  actually losing time, rather than guessing.
- Making the platform AI-native — MCP servers and skills that let AI agents
  work safely against enterprise CI/CD systems.
- Large migrations that land on schedule, such as moving 10,000+ repositories
  from GitHub Enterprise Server to GitHub Enterprise Cloud in under a year.

## Career arc

He has spent his whole career so far at The Hartford, starting in production
support in 2016 and moving through reliability engineering, full-stack
development, batch modernization and quality engineering before taking on
enterprise platform engineering. That path is why he cares about platforms:
he spent years as the engineer the platform was supposed to serve.

## Outside the day job

- Organizes The Hartford's enterprise hackathons, which draw hundreds of
  engineers a year.
- Mentors associates in the company's Tech & Operations Leadership
  Development Program.
- Sits on the Central Connecticut State University computer science industry
  advisory board, judging senior projects and helping shape the curriculum.
- Builds side projects (listed separately) and runs a homelab with Proxmox,
  Docker, Tailscale and local LLM inference through Ollama.

## How he works

- Writes down why decisions went the way they did — his projects keep
  decision logs, and his design system has a DECISIONS.md.
- Prefers honest numbers: the figures on his site are current and documented,
  and he would rather understate than oversell.
- Builds for himself first, to scratch a real itch, then shares what's useful.
